04 · When and where

Pedestrians peak in winter, cyclists in the warmer months

Pedestrian hospitalisations rise from May to August; cyclist hospitalisations are highest in March and October and lowest in July. The data records the month, not the time of day, so darker evenings are a possible explanation, not a finding.

About the data

  • A hospitalisation is not a person. BITRE counts hospital separations; one person can be counted more than once.
  • No fault. The data records what the injured person collided with, not who caused the crash.
  • Series breaks. Victoria (from 2012) and NSW (from 2017) changed what counts as an admission, so 2012 and 2019 are used as comparison years.
  • COVID. 2020 and 2021 travel was unusual and is shaded or excluded.
  • No exposure data. We do not know how many people walk or cycle, so a rise in cyclist injuries may partly reflect more cycling.
  • Who is missing. People killed at the scene are not in hospital data. Patients who died within 30 days are reported separately.
  • Scope. First Nations data was left out: a narrow treatment without community context risked misrepresenting First Nations people.

Data: Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (2023), Hospitalised injury; Australian Bureau of Statistics, Regional population by age and sex (LGA estimates). Prototype figures computed with scripts/build_data.py; final figures must come from the team's KNIME workflow.
